Migrate by business-use product, not by API inventory.

Executive answer

Use Apigee X as the new governed API operating model, and move one business usage grouping at a time through a repeatable migration factory. Keep Akana live only as a controlled coexistence path through the external load balancer.

Situation

Akana runs critical APIs.

The current gateway estate supports business capabilities, but the implementation has grown around delivery pressure rather than deliberate platform design.

Complication

The design debt is structural.

Poor product taxonomy, inconsistent gateway patterns, tight coupling, manual deployments, and weak governance make a bulk migration risky.

Question

How do we migrate without moving the mess?

Move the business capability, consumers, policies, certificates, and onboarding model together, rather than translating individual proxies.

Answer

Use product waves.

Each Apigee Product becomes a governed migration unit with explicit consumers, identity model, SLAs, traffic path, ownership, and retirement criteria.

Decision required: endorse product-led migration as the default unit of change MECE lens: platform, product, consumer, operations, risk

Implementation Approach

Run a repeatable migration factory for each Apigee Product.

04 / 10
01

Discover

Map Akana APIs, consumers, apps, certificates, policies, traffic, SLAs, incidents, and manual release steps for the product use case.

02

Design

Define Apigee Product, proxy boundaries, quota model, Ping token pattern, cert model, error contract, analytics tags, and ownership.

03

Build

Implement proxies using approved templates: auth, threat protection, mediation, routing, logging, response handling, and reusable policies.

04

Prove

Run contract, security, performance, certificate, Ping, rollback, observability, and consumer regression validation before onboarding.

05

Onboard

Create developer portal product, apps, credentials, subscriptions, and runbook-backed support path for each consumer group.

06

Cutover

Shift external load balancer routing by product, monitor agreed service signals, and keep rollback window time-bound.

07

Retire

Remove Akana routes, decommission credentials, revoke unused certs, archive configs, and update product inventory.

Recommendation: the first product should be meaningful but bounded: enough consumers and policies to prove the platform pattern, not the highest-risk business flow.
Exit rule: no product proceeds without owner, consumers, SLA, identity, certs, test evidence, and rollback path Factory creates speed through repetition

Foundation Controls

Build six non-negotiable controls before scaling beyond the first product.

06 / 10
Identity

Ping integration standard

Use a defined OAuth/OIDC pattern: issuer validation, audience, claims, scopes, token lifetime, introspection or JWT validation, and service identity expectations.

Certificates

Trust lifecycle

Document mTLS usage, keystore/truststore ownership, rotation calendar, chain validation, break-glass process, and certificate retirement on Akana decommission.

CI/CD

Template-based promotion

Promote config and proxy bundles through environments using automated checks, parameterized config, policy linting, and approval gates for risk-sensitive changes.

Runtime

Policy catalog

Create sanctioned recipes for auth, quota, spike arrest, threat protection, mediation, target routing, error mapping, logging, and analytics dimensions.

Portal

Consumer onboarding

Use developer portal product pages, app registration, approval workflow, credential issuance, subscription visibility, and product-specific support path.

Governance

Architecture board

Approve exceptions, avoid pattern drift, track technical debt, validate readiness evidence, and enforce Akana retirement after each product cutover.

Recommendation: scale only when the foundation is real enough to stop bespoke implementations Controls are practical gates, not governance theatre

Challenges, Pitfalls, and Prevention

Most migration failures come from unmanaged coupling, not Apigee technology.

08 / 10
Issue one might face
Prevention recommendation
Bad Akana taxonomy gets recreated. Teams lift and shift proxy groupings.
Require product design approval based on business use case, consumer segment, SLA, owner, and lifecycle before any build starts.
Consumers are discovered late. Hidden apps depend on legacy routes or certificates.
Use Akana logs, LB logs, cert inventory, app registry, and support data to build a consumer evidence pack per product.
Identity scope drift. Ping claims and Apigee policies are interpreted differently per team.
Create a Ping-to-Apigee auth contract: issuers, audiences, scopes, claims, token handling, and rejected-token behavior.
Manual deployment habits continue. Apigee becomes another manually changed runtime.
Make CI/CD the only standard path. Exceptions require time-bound architecture approval and post-change reconciliation.
Coexistence becomes permanent. Akana routes stay open after cutover.
Define decommission gates up front: traffic zero, consumers moved, certs revoked, route removed, config archived, ownership closed.

Recommended governance cadence

Weekly product wave review: readiness, open decisions, risk burn-down, consumer onboarding, and route retirement.

Fortnightly architecture board: exceptions, new patterns, policy catalog, Ping model, cert model, and cross-product dependencies.

Monthly executive checkpoint: migrated business capability, Akana dependency retired, operational risk reduced, and next product approved.

Final rule: every migration must leave the platform simpler than it found it.

Last recommendation: protect product taxonomy, automation, identity, and retirement discipline from day one Pitfalls playbook

How To Execute

Start with a platform runway, then migrate products in evidence-based waves.

Weeks 0-4

Build the runway

Set up Apigee X environments, shared VPC routing, external load balancer path, CI/CD skeleton, Ping validation pattern, certificate model, and policy catalog.

Output: deployable platform baseline
Weeks 4-8

Prove Product 1

Select a bounded product with real consumers. Complete discovery, design, build, test, portal onboarding, controlled cutover, and Akana retirement.

Output: first reusable migration pattern
Weeks 8-16

Industrialize waves

Run two or three additional products through the factory, tighten templates, remove exceptions, and prove repeatability across different consumer types.

Output: product migration playbook
Beyond 16

Scale and retire

Prioritize the remaining portfolio by business value, risk, dependency complexity, Akana cost, and consumer readiness. Retire Akana routes as a managed KPI.

Output: governed migration portfolio
01

Choose the first product carefully

Pick a product that is visible enough to prove value but bounded enough to avoid making the first wave a rescue mission.

02

Make patterns reusable before speed

Every shortcut in Product 1 becomes inherited debt. Prioritize templates for auth, errors, logging, certs, deployment, and portal onboarding.

03

Keep coexistence visibly temporary

The load balancer should route by migration state, not become a permanent abstraction that hides Akana dependency.

04

Fund the operating model

Budget for product ownership, platform engineering, consumer onboarding, release automation, and governance capacity, not only proxy conversion.

Executive Recommendations

Lock six decisions before scaling the migration.

10 / 10
Decide now Confirm product-led migration, target architecture, ownership model, funding, and the first product wave.
Control every wave Use exit gates for security, Ping, certificates, CI/CD, portal onboarding, observability, rollback, and Akana retirement.
Measure what matters Track retired Akana traffic, onboarded consumers, automated releases, open exceptions, operational incidents, and platform reuse.

1. Product taxonomy

Approve a business-use taxonomy and reject technical lift-and-shift groupings unless an explicit exception is granted.

2. First-wave scope

Choose one bounded product with known consumers, visible value, manageable policy complexity, and a credible rollback path.

3. Platform guardrails

Mandate standard templates for Ping auth, mTLS, quotas, threat protection, logging, error contracts, and environment promotion.

4. Consumer migration

Treat app onboarding as part of migration scope: app records, credentials, subscriptions, portal docs, support, and communications.

5. Retirement discipline

Make Akana route retirement a formal completion metric, with certificate revocation and config archival included in done.

6. Exception budget

Limit exceptions by wave, assign owners and expiry dates, and review unresolved exceptions at the executive checkpoint.

Final recommendation: do not migrate the gateway; migrate the API operating model and retire Akana product by product End of deck
